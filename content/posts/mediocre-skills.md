I recently read (sight book here) that there is much value in building mastery to skills.
I should cite what I read (Atomic Habits), but the author mentions the creator of Dilbert, the newspaper comic.
He worked in IT or something and is credited for his dry humor and cartoons conveying life of an IT manager.
That drawings are neat but nothing spectacular. He claims he is not funny enough to be a comedian and also not good enough at drawing to be an artist.
However, he has combined there two better than average skills of being funning and a decent cartoonist and has made something rare and valuable to many people.

I do not know that I will ever be able to be the greatest at any of the skills that I can currently persuing. I want to be a great baroque-style pianist, a polyglot and a polymath.
The last two skills mentioned are probably to broad and I could do with narrowing their scope. Anyway, here are some of the skills that I have that are not awesome, but decent.

* I am good at math and compsci (not research level but I can apply math to problems and write programs)
* I am good at MS Excel (I can do some light programming, optimization, computation and analysis)
* I know a little bit of several languages (romance and north-indian)
* I am getting better at writing largely due to learning linguistic principles through languages
* Finally, I can play some music (a little guitar and I am really focusing on baroque keyboard)

That is pretty much it. Any other skills I have are probably not worth mentioning.
I would like to combine these into something and maybe I cannot. But at least thinking about it could be a good exercise to try to narrow down my focus.
I am currently reading McKeown's Essentialism and it is really emphasizing doing less. And I have been on this journey of doing less for a while now.
For example I have mostly put away the guitar in favor of piano. I just find it is the best one for me to express my musical ideas (see my post on musical growth).
But that's about all the paring down I have done so far. I am perhaps narrowing down on language but not well. The languages are all related however: French, Spanish, Italian, Hindi, Gujarati.
German and Dutch have also been interests. What else?

As far as writing goes, I just need to do it more. And read more. Those are both simple remedies for improving. For even fast improvement, I should discren the kind of writing I want to do.
After this, I can look for specific examples of that sort of writing (pros, fiction, non-fiction, etc.) and improve that way.
After all, in Slow Productivity, Cal Newport recounts that the best chess players do not just play as much as possible but rather zero in on the examples,
plays and cases that come up so that they can catalog the best moves.
Relatedly, when learning a musical piece, it is most efficient to break it down and work on each part in a manner of spaced repetition, spending the most time on the parts that are not sticking.
Further, when learning a language spaced repetition with focus on new material is indispensible (along with many other methods).
I guess what I am saying, is you want to give your brain the best chance possible to remember. And both memory and memorization are important.
Do not shy away from memorizing to the extent that you understand what you have memorized. After all, there is a difference between a parrot and a person.
This memorization gives you license to focus on "higher level" building blocks since the details have been naturally sorted by your memorization and intuition.
